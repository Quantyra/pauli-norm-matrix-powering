Antigravity suggested a formula for the late edge. The gate marker has time \(aD+i\) at offset \(i\) in sweep \(a\). The last gate starts at offset \(n-2\), hence \(g=5D+n-2\), where \(D=n(2n+3)\). Its proposed index \(5D+2(n-1)\) is incorrect. The revised paper gives the verified index and identifies the edge it labels. Other shorthand in its report also needs qualification. Two identity **transitions** separate successive work gates, the YES gate is the last gate of sweep 5, and the endpoint-walk comparison refers to the Janzing–Wocjan mixing paper. The signed mean represents the matrix diagonal for ideal draws. The capped testing bound uses domination of surviving paths. Writing the Pauli strings themselves costs \(O(n^2)\) letters across the list, dominated by the unary power of order \(n^5\). [Clock schedule](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0705.4077) · [Janzing–Wocjan comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610235)

We checked AGIK's original graphical rules 1–6, boundary conventions and Figure 1. They give seven terms on a within-block bond, six across a boundary, two open-end terms and the extra terminal pin in this construction. The two conditional probabilities for the quantum source and the rounded-input decision margins are established in Appendix A. At width 42 the unary power is 36,424,602,825,523,200. The revision explains that the finite verifier constructs local rows without instantiating that many steps. We retained the width congruence. The suggested arbitrary-width grid need not be the largest grid permitted by Appendix A's rule. The variable edge factor \(a_t\) already allows a distinct scale at each Hadamard. [AGIK rules and figure, pp. 10–11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0705.4077)

The work string consists of the specified \(q\) Hadamard bits. All other data have the prescribed padding, control and output values. The proof requires row equality only before the first visit to the late neighborhood. No assertion about the smallest divergence set is needed. The two ordinary masks that coincide cause only sums of grid values. Width 42 works up to \(q=12\), and the next admissible width is 87,652,394. Larger widths are chosen as needed for the asymptotic family.

Theorem 5 gives a single-trial variance obstruction for every full-support path proposal and allowed rounding on its witness. Theorem 6 uses the specified absolute-row proposal and a chosen common rounding on a separate pair. The \(2^q/3\) lower bound concerns decoders that observe only independent scalar scores. The source decisions are directly readable from the changed circuit gate.

All three reviewers returned READY on the final source with no mandatory corrections. Two phrases in Grok's report require the manuscript's precise hypotheses. A diagonal correction on the first two sites is scalar on each reached layer. An arbitrary operator supported there could change its tags. Under a given cap, the avoiding-path score distribution is common to the two hypotheses. Its mass can differ from the ideal experiment's. Neither generalization is used in the proof.
