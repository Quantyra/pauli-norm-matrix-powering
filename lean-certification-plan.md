# Lean certification plan

This plan covers Theorems 1, 3, 5 and 6 and Corollaries 2, 3.1 and 4 in the [v1.6.0 manuscript](https://github.com/Quantyra/pauli-norm-matrix-powering/blob/v1.6.0/manuscript.tex). Pin the tagged source and the digest in the [review disposition](review/v1.6.0/disposition.md) at the start of certification. Each stage has its own acceptance test. Formal certification is prospective.

## Parameters and interfaces

Fix a real cap \(c>1\) **outside** the reduction. Choose a natural number \(h\ge1\) with \(d=2^{-h}\le c-1\). The encoded problem gives the positive power in unary and fixes the two thresholds. The hardness reduction hardwires \(h\). The all-input estimator can use the collected coefficient sum directly. Use \(K=2M\), \(s=d/K\), \(M\) a power of two at least \(4\), \(m=6M^3/d\), \(g=1/4\) and dyadic margin \(1/32\). Encode \(\tau\) and the rounding grid by integer bit lengths. Record separately the comparator that handles \(0<c\le1\).

Use finite work and clock basis types with a chosen zero-clock vector. Use complex Euclidean space for the operator norm, with matrix entries obtained by applying an endomorphism to the basis vectors. The Pauli coefficient norm is a sum of absolute coefficients on distinct, canonical Pauli labels, including the identity. Establish the operator-norm bound by this sum. Using the Frobenius norm or induced column-sum norm here would change the theorem. Use exact dyadic encodings on the output tape; complex algebraic coefficients appear only in the intermediate reduction.

## Corollary 2 on the spin chain

1. Encode the local tags in four qubits per site. Prove in both directions that the modified first bond acts only between the marked start and its successor on the spectator-zero computation, and prove the one-site reject projector acts only at the terminal shape. An extra transition on a reachable shape falsifies this stage.
2. Construct the legal-history isometry and its intertwining relation for arbitrary nonspectator data. Recover the zero-energy weight \(p/2\) and prove the accepting and terminally pinned rejecting forms have positive spectrum at least \(1/(6T^2)\) on the visible complement. Track the global operator bounds separately from the gap on the legal subspace.
3. Produce the position-dependent two-site Pauli list, certify the coefficient intervals and prove the rounded contraction bounds on the full Hilbert space. With \(s=d/(2K)\) and \(m=72KT^2/d\), reuse stages A and E for the fixed-error decision. Check locality after collecting and rounding labels, the unary output cost and both final margins.

The finite rewrite and rational-form examples in `verify_chain.py` provide regression cases. The history isometry and the gap admit independent formalization. The complete complexity statement also requires the filtering and algorithmic interfaces in A and E.

## Theorem 3 and Corollary 4 on weighted sampling

1. For a finite sign sum, prove the Chebyshev identity, the truncation error below \(1/128\), and the weighted recurrence after blocking. Transport a uniform scalar error to a basis diagonal of any Hermitian contraction. Implement the dyadic coefficient tables, signed Pauli draws and capped retries, with polynomial bit cost per trial and total failure at most \(1/4\).
2. Translate a polynomial about one and prove the binomial bound on the Taylor tail. Rescale Markov's derivative inequality on the short interval ending at one. Derive the lower bound for every polynomial degree, including complex coefficients by taking real parts. Its conclusion concerns coefficient mass.
3. For Corollary 4, use the installed local projectors to verify their normalized trace bound and the alternating-word energy on the full space. Propagate the coefficient error of the actual rounded matrix, derive \(m\ln\lambda=\Theta(NT^2)\) and instantiate the specified sampler's trial count. An operator-norm perturbation alone cannot certify the required lower bound on Pauli weight.

## Appendix B on variance at the marked input

First formalize the finite path identity for the absolute score of a full-support proposal and the diagonal-word contribution to the Pauli trial. Then show that a real symmetric principal submatrix with top eigenvalue at least \(1+\gamma s\), a bounded-length connector from \(j\), and an even remaining power gives the stated diagonal growth. The application requires reconstructing the three-wire clock in the computational data basis, bounding rounded entries before taking magnitudes, and paying both connector weights. The first certificate could cover the abstract finite-path lemma. Certification of Theorem 5 also requires proving those concrete clock hypotheses. Track ideal trials and capped rejection separately.

## Theorem 6 on fixed-confidence testing

1. For two finite score laws, formalize the common mass from paths avoiding a set and the shared point mass at zero after a visit. Deduce a total variation bound from the two exceptional nonzero masses. Prove the independent product bound and its consequence for randomized decisions. Capped paths require a separate subprobability domination lemma.
2. Prove the rounded matrix has the indicated work-string blocks. Check the modular sequence of seven-sweep widths, exact ordinary grid multiples, local Pauli mask separation, marked boundary support and preservation of legal layers after collection. A wrong mask, hidden work dependence in the first two sites or an off-history exit invalidates this application.
3. Square the block matrix before taking row magnitudes. Factor the finite trajectory law into clock and data kernels. Given a complete returning clock path that visited the late gate, prove the last crossings of all \(q\) cuts reset to independent fair bits. Combine these results with the two source promises and the decoder's observation contract. A certificate for the abstract testing inequality alone leaves the rounded-input theorem open.

## Corollary 3.1 and the commuting-remainder comparison

For Corollary 3.1, reuse the scalar filter and capped sampling stages. The following obligations can be certified separately.

1. Identify deletion sets with weighted vertex covers on collected Pauli labels. Certify the dual charge invariant, factor two, tableau elimination, and inclusion of every diagonal term. Prove that pinching gives a contraction on the full space. Track the cost of constructing the conflict graph and the Clifford basis from explicit dyadics.
2. For a stabilizer tableau, construct affine support, sampling map and signed translation lifts. Prove the conjugate endpoint identity and the unbiased mean for each degree, including zero diagonal factors and endpoints outside the support. Use a state with imaginary relative phase to distinguish the two possible conjugation conventions. Combine with the scalar filter and carry the retry, bit, space and success bounds through the signed dyadic accumulator.
3. On the growing two-local family certify the optimum cover, the common eigenvector, the deletion gap and the absolute row bounds on both powers. Reuse the return-score second-moment lemma for the ideal walks. For cap-only hardness reuse the cyclic reduction at \(d=1/4\) and its strict rounded margins, then bound every collected coefficient by one to recover all-input quantum membership. Separate the finite operator identities from any eventual costed complexity certificate.

## First allocation and handback

Begin with a 90-minute dependency and statement audit of A. Use Lean 4.33.0 and mathlib revision `db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d` as a proposed pin; the upstream toolchain file specifies that Lean release. Check the local environment before allocating a build. First implement a scalar finite-sum inequality using an explicit finite list of positive weights, including the possibility that the top atom has zero mass. Test whether the matrix-power perturbation bound can be transported to one basis diagonal in the operator norm. Return a compiling conditional lemma or a dependency report with the exact missing types. Stop this tranche if the norm is mismatched or if a spectral or complexity conclusion is assumed at its own interface. The scalar stage is the smallest useful certificate. The cyclic Fourier decomposition and the all-input complexity formalization are the largest uncertainties. Estimate those after the dependency audit. For each accepted stage archive the frozen paper and source revision, toolchain, clean build instructions, declaration types, theorem-to-manuscript mapping and transitive axiom report. Check for `sorry`, `admit` and custom axioms. Permitted foundational axioms are `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Report conditional estimates, matrix and circuit results, a costed reduction, and full complexity-class certification at their actual coverage.
