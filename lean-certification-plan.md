# Lean certification plan

The target is Theorem 1 of [the reviewed source](https://github.com/Quantyra/pauli-norm-matrix-powering/blob/988f17351b893e803dce674df7e304ec8f345fb4/manuscript.tex), SHA-256 `40fa75257749535764f800cc894bc1a05284393a94d758fcf8eea0aca351742c`. Pin that file when beginning a certificate. The theorem has two computational claims and a finite-bit hardness construction. Each stage below has its own acceptance test. This plan is prospective; the package contains no kernel-checked proof.

## Parameters and interfaces

Fix a real cap \(c>1\) **outside** the reduction. Choose a natural number \(h\ge1\) with \(d=2^{-h}\le c-1\). The encoded problem gives the positive power and reciprocal tolerance in unary. The uniform input algorithm uses hard-coded \(h\) and never reads an oracle for \(c\). Use \(K=2M\), \(s=d/K\), \(M\) a power of two at least \(4\), \(m=M^2\lceil\log_2(64M)\rceil/(2s)\), \(g=1/(2M)\) and dyadic margin \(1/(16M)\). Encode \(\tau\) and the rounding grid by integer bit lengths. Record separately the comparator that handles \(0<c\le1\).

Use finite work and clock basis types with a chosen zero-clock vector. Use complex Euclidean space for the operator norm, with matrix entries obtained by applying an endomorphism to the basis vectors. The Pauli coefficient norm is a sum of absolute coefficients on distinct, canonical Pauli labels, including the identity. Establish the operator-norm bound by this sum. Using the Frobenius norm or induced column-sum norm here would change the theorem. Use exact dyadic encodings on the output tape; complex algebraic coefficients appear only in the intermediate reduction.

## Stages and acceptance criteria

| Stage | Exact obligation | Evidence and first falsifier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Filter and rounding | For a finite probability measure supported on one top eigenvalue and a gapped complement, prove the upper and lower bounds on its shifted \(m\)-th moment. Then prove the power telescoping inequality for two contractions and transport the rounded moment into the YES/NO threshold. | Reproduce the \(1/(64M)\) leakage, \(3/(256M)\) rounding allowance, and the promise margin with the manuscript's constants. Test the signs at \(p=0,1\). This stage may take the atom mass and gap as explicit hypotheses. |
| B. Clock spectrum | From a list of unitary gates on a cyclic register with \(M\) labels, prove full-space unitarity. Decompose the clock into periodic and twisted rings of the input reflection \(C^\dagger(2\Pi-I)C\). Obtain the squared projection mass \(p/M\) and the complement's gap of at least \(2/M^2\). | Supply the Fourier normalization, reflection orientation and both sectors including zero projections. A proof only on a legal subspace needs an explicit theorem discharging the global contraction promise. |
| C. Finite Pauli output | Enumerate the binary matrix units and bounded-arity gate decompositions. Prove \(O(M^2)\) terms, coefficient weight at most \(2M\), and the fixed-cap bound after mixing. Give exact sign comparison and directed rounding for dyadic pairs \(u+v\sqrt2\), preserving positivity and both norms after inward scaling. | Verify the identity term, conjugation and duplicate collection, and charge the written labels and grid bits. Check that states with no support on the tested input still satisfy the operator promise. |
| D. Reduction | Assemble A–C as an actual classical map from an encoded bounded-error circuit to the encoded threshold instance, valid on all source YES and NO inputs. Prove bit time and output length polynomial for each fixed \(h\). | Acceptance must imply the correct side of \(g\), including source compilation slack, optional qubit padding, and the position of \(m\) in the input encoding. Supplying the acceptance probability to the construction invalidates the reduction. |
| E. Algorithms and class statement | Formalize or import a proved Pauli-product estimator on the unit ball, finite random-bit sampling, concentration, and the quantum spectral estimator for every promised contraction. Compose with D in the chosen PromiseBQP model. | Capture the actual assumptions on phase-estimation error, Hamiltonian simulation, uniform circuit synthesis and classical reductions. A missing complexity library remains an explicit dependency. A–D give only their stated coverage until these interfaces compile and match the paper. |

## First allocation and handback

Begin with a 90-minute dependency and statement audit of A. Use Lean 4.33.0 and mathlib revision `db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d` as a proposed pin; the upstream toolchain file specifies that Lean release. Check the local environment before allocating a build. First implement a scalar finite-sum inequality using an explicit finite list of positive weights, including the possibility that the top atom has zero mass. Test whether the matrix-power perturbation bound can be transported to one basis diagonal in the operator norm. Return a compiling conditional lemma or a dependency report with the exact missing types. Stop this tranche if the norm is mismatched or if a spectral or complexity conclusion is assumed at its own interface. [Pinned upstream toolchain](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d/lean-toolchain)

The scalar stage is the smallest useful certificate. The cyclic Fourier decomposition and the all-input complexity formalization are the largest uncertainties. Estimate those after the dependency audit. For each accepted stage archive the frozen paper and source revision, toolchain, clean build instructions, declaration types, theorem-to-manuscript mapping and transitive axiom report. Check for `sorry`, `admit` and custom axioms. Permitted foundational axioms are `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Report conditional estimates, matrix and circuit results, a costed reduction, and full complexity-class certification at their actual coverage.
